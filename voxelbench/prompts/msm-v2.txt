Task:
Design and create an animated voxel diorama of Mont-Saint-Michel in Normandy, France, as the tide comes in. Make it detailed, impressive and varied, with colourful voxels and great attention to detail. It must include:
1. The mount: a granite rock rising to a peak in the middle of the bay, not a flat island, with varied grey and brown stone and a few patches of green on its ledges.
2. The abbey on the summit: Gothic walls, the church, and a slender spire topped by a golden statue of Saint Michael that catches the light.
3. The village: medieval ramparts with towers around the base, and houses with pitched roofs climbing the slope along one main street.
4. The bay: wide sand flats cut by channels, and a tide that visibly rises to surround the mount and falls again, on a loop of about one minute. The water is animated, slightly transparent, takes its colour from the sky, and shows a line of white foam where it meets the sand.
5. The causeway from the mainland, with pilgrims and visitors walking along it and across the sands at low tide. They must be large enough to see from the default camera.
6. Salt meadows on the mainland side in several shades of green, with tufts and small flowers, and sheep that are recognisable at a glance (white woolly bodies, black faces and legs) grazing and wandering slowly.
7. The sky: flocks of seagulls flapping their wings, clouds drifting high above the abbey spire that never pass through the mount or the buildings, a visible sun that moves across the sky and casts soft shadows with warm golden light at sunrise and sunset, and a passing rain shower with realistic weather physics. At night show a moon and stars, with enough cool moonlight that the whole scene stays readable, and warm glowing windows and street lamps in the village.
8. Interaction: orbit the camera with the mouse and zoom with the wheel; press T to speed up the tide, W to start or stop the weather, N to switch between day and night. The default camera frames the whole bay and the mount.
Use WebGL and whatever libraries you need, but make sure it all fits in a single HTML file I can open in Chrome. It must run smoothly with no console errors.
Save the final file as index.html in the current working directory.
