Use Goal Mode. You have about one hour of wall time and no one to ask. Verify your work.

Task: write a chess engine that speaks the UCI protocol, in ONE Python 3 file. Save the final file as engine.py in the current working directory (the exact path is given at the end of this message).

Rules of the task:
1. Python 3 standard library only (the interpreter is `python3`). No pip install, no python-chess, no other chess library, no copy of any other engine, no network access, no subprocess or os.system, no calling another program. Write the move generator and the search yourself from your own knowledge. A referee you cannot see reads your file and rejects an engine that imports anything outside the standard library or touches the network or other programs.
2. Work in engine.py from your very first write. Never split the engine into several source files. Get a correct board and legal move generator running first, then the UCI shell, then a search, then make it stronger one feature at a time, so a working engine.py exists at every moment. Short test scripts of your own in the working directory are fine.
3. Chess rules must be complete: castling (both sides, with the rights lost when the king or a rook moves, no castling out of, through or into check), en passant, promotion to queen, rook, bishop and knight (UCI move text such as e7e8q), check, checkmate and stalemate. Your engine never has to claim a draw, but it must always answer `go` with a legal move when one exists.
4. UCI commands to support: `uci` (reply with `id name <name>`, `id author <name>`, `uciok`), `isready` (reply `readyok`), `ucinewgame`, `position startpos [moves m1 m2 ...]` and `position fen <fen> [moves ...]`, `go movetime <ms>`, `go depth <n>`, `go wtime <ms> btime <ms> [winc <ms> binc <ms>]`, `quit`. Answer every `go` with exactly one `bestmove <move>` line. Print `info depth <d> score cp <n> nodes <n> time <ms> pv <move>` lines while searching (use `score mate <n>` for forced mates). Flush stdout after every line.
5. Time: when asked for `go movetime N`, send the bestmove within N milliseconds (a small margin is fine, but a move that arrives late loses the game). Use iterative deepening and check the clock inside the search.
6. Perft: also support `go perft <n>`, the debugging command other engines have: from the current position, print one line `<move>: <count>` per legal root move, then an empty line, then `Nodes searched: <total>`, where the counts are the number of leaf positions n plies deep. The move generator is checked with these counts to depth 5 from the starting position, and the whole depth-5 run must finish in under 20 minutes, so keep the generator reasonably quick.
7. After the engine works it will play real games against other engines at a fixed time per move. An illegal move, a crash or a late move loses the game. Stronger play wins.

Check your own work as you go: run the engine through a pipe (`printf 'uci\nisready\nposition startpos\ngo movetime 500\nquit\n' | python3 engine.py`), test perft on positions whose counts you know, and play it against itself or a simple random mover of your own to catch illegal moves. Stop when the engine is solid; a correct engine beats a clever broken one.
